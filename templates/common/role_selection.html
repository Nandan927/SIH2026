What it is

What is Skill Gap Analysis?

Skill gap analysis is the systematic practice of identifying the distance between what someone can do today and what their role, team, or industry will require tomorrow. It replaces guesswork with measurable evidence. Instead of assuming a designer can lead a design system, or that a salesperson can pitch a new product line, you map the specific competencies each role demands — and then you check, honestly, which of those competencies the person actually has.

The power of this approach comes from treating skills as data. Every role becomes a set of requirements: technical proficiencies, tool familiarity, communication abilities, domain knowledge, leadership behaviours. Every individual becomes a profile: verified skills, self-reported skills, latent strengths, demonstrated weaknesses. When you overlay these two maps, the gaps — the missing competencies — become visible, quantified, and prioritized.

This matters at every scale. For an individual, it means a career path that is actually grounded in where they are weak, not in generic advice. For a team manager, it means knowing precisely who needs what before a project starts. For an organisation, it means training budgets that go where they close the largest gaps — not where vendors happen to be selling courses.

Horizon was designed around a simple insight: the fastest way to grow people is to stop treating learning as a fixed curriculum and start treating it as a closing of the gap. Every recommendation you'll see on this platform traces back to a specific, identified skill delta — nothing generic, nothing wasted.

The core idea: a gap only becomes actionable once it is named, measured, and connected to a pathway that closes it. That's what Horizon does.
